CATEGORY 1: INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY & COMPUTING
BSIT-001 — BS Information Technology

Overview: BS Information Technology is a broad, hands-on tech course that trains students to install, manage, and troubleshoot the computer systems organizations depend on every day. It's not limited to one specialization — depending on the electives and specialization track a school offers, IT graduates can move into network administration, cybersecurity, systems/database administration, technical support, UI/UX design, or entry-level software and web development. The course emphasizes applying and maintaining existing technology to solve real business problems, making it a practical entry point into almost any tech-related career.

Guidance Tip: 
Free introductory resources such as HTML and CSS tutorials on YouTube or Sololearn can help students get a feel for the technical side of IT early on. Some students only realize later that IT work tends to be logical and repetitive rather than exciting, so this kind of early exposure is worth the time. Once in college, TESDA or vendor certifications such as CompTIA or Cisco NetAcad can strengthen a graduate's chances when applying for a first job.

BSCS-001 — BS Computer Science

Overview: Computer Science is the theory-heavy foundation of software — algorithms, data structures, and the math and logic behind how programs actually work. It suits students who enjoy problem-solving and puzzles more than just "liking computers" in general. Beyond traditional software development, a CS background opens doors into data science and machine learning, competitive/algorithmic programming roles, game development, and research-oriented tech careers, since the theoretical foundation transfers well across specializations.

CATEGORY 2: ENGINEERING & TECHNOLOGY
BSCE-001 — BS Civil Engineering

Overview: Civil Engineering trains students to design, build, and maintain the physical infrastructure people rely on daily — roads, bridges, buildings, drainage, and water systems. Coursework covers structural design, materials, surveying, and project planning. Beyond construction sites, civil engineers also work in project management, government infrastructure planning, quantity surveying/cost estimation, and disaster-resilient design — an increasingly relevant specialization given the region's typhoon exposure. The program ends in a licensure exam required to legally sign off on construction plans.

It builds strong skills in lab procedures, quality control, and accuracy, since lab result errors can directly affect patient diagnosis. Beyond hospital labs, graduates can also work in research laboratories, blood banks, and public health/epidemiology testing programs.

Where Can You Study:

Guidance Tip: 
This field demands precision and patience. Being detail-oriented matters as much as academic performance, so paying attention to how careful, repetitive tasks are handled now is useful preparation. Passing the Med Tech Licensure Exam is required, and a strong Biology and Chemistry background makes the coursework noticeably more manageable.

BSRT-001 — BS Radiologic Technology

Overview: This course trains students to operate imaging equipment — X-ray, CT scan, and MRI machines — used to help diagnose illness and injury. Graduates work closely with radiologists and physicians, with training that emphasizes both technical precision and patient safety, since this equipment involves radiation that must be handled carefully. Beyond hospital imaging departments, some graduates move into specialized imaging centers or radiation therapy support roles.

CATEGORY 6: ARCHITECTURE, FINE ARTS & DESIGN
BSArch-001 — BS Architecture

Overview: Architecture trains students to design buildings and spaces, blending creative design thinking with technical knowledge of structures, materials, and safety codes. It's a five-year program — longer than most bachelor's degrees — ending in a licensure exam administered by the PRC. Beyond designing buildings, graduates can move into urban and land-use planning, construction project management, 3D visualization/rendering, heritage conservation work, and (increasingly) disaster-resilient design, given the region's typhoon exposure. A strong, ever-growing design portfolio matters as much as grades, even for landing an internship.

Where Can You Study:

Bicol University Legazpi (Institute of Design and Architecture), Legazpi City, Albay — Public, free tuition under RA 10931
Camarines Sur Polytechnic Colleges (CSPC, College of Engineering and Architecture), Nabua, Camarines Sur — Public, free tuition under RA 10931
Ateneo de Naga University (College of Science, Engineering, and Architecture; program opened 2022), Naga City, Camarines Sur — Private
Elsewhere: UST, UP, and Mapúa are well known nationally for Architecture.
Note: No community college in Albay currently offers the full 5-year BS Architecture program. TESDA's Architectural Drafting NC II is a shorter, non-licensure course that can build relevant drafting skills at low cost, but it is not a substitute for the degree or the board exam.

Where Can This Path Lead:

Junior Architect/Architectural Drafter — ₱20,000–₱32,000/mo entry-level
3D Visualizer/Renderer — ₱18,000–₱28,000/mo entry-level, often freelance-friendly
Construction Project Coordinator — ₱20,000–₱30,000/mo entry-level, common in design-and-build firms
Government Architect I — ₱33,947/mo (Salary Grade 12, once licensed and hired in an LGU or government engineering office)
Interior/Space Planning Consultant (typically a later-career path, post-licensure) — variable income

Guidance Tip: This program is a bigger commitment than most courses, given the five-year timeline and licensure exam, so it should be a genuine choice rather than something chosen for its prestige. Building a design portfolio now, even through simple sketches or digital design projects, matters since schools and later employers look at this closely.

BFA-001 — Bachelor of Fine Arts

Overview: Fine Arts develops skills in painting, sculpture, and other visual art forms through heavy studio practice, alongside art history and theory. It's a broader career path than "just painting" — graduates move into gallery and exhibition work, art direction for advertising and media, art education, illustration, and increasingly, digital/commission-based art through online platforms. Success is measured largely through a body of work rather than grades, since a portfolio is what actually opens doors to galleries, agencies, or teaching positions later. It suits students with a genuine, sustained interest in creating art rather than those who enjoy art only as a hobby.

Where Can You Study:

UP Los Baños/UP Diliman (College of Fine Arts) — Public, national reference, free tuition under RA 10931
Ateneo de Naga University (Digital Illustration and Animation, under a related design program), Naga City, Camarines Sur — Private
Elsewhere: University of Santo Tomas College of Fine Arts and Design is a well-known national option.
Note: No community college in Albay or Camarines Sur currently offers a BFA program. Many working Filipino artists supplement or replace formal BFA training with TESDA's Visual Graphic Design NC III, free online courses, or informal mentorship/apprenticeship under a working artist — a realistic lower-cost path worth considering if a BFA program isn't locally accessible.

Where Can This Path Lead:

Freelance Illustrator/Artist — variable, commission-based income, especially in the first few years
Art Teacher/Studio Instructor — ₱15,000–₱22,000/mo entry-level
Gallery/Exhibition Assistant or Curator's Assistant — ₱15,000–₱20,000/mo entry-level
Advertising/Editorial Art Director (junior) — ₱18,000–₱28,000/mo entry-level, common for graduates who lean toward commercial art
Concept Artist/Illustrator for games or publishing — variable income, often freelance or contract-based, increasingly accessible through online platforms

Guidance Tip: A strong, distinct portfolio matters more than grades in this field, so building an online presence early — even a simple Instagram or ArtStation account — helps with getting comfortable sharing work and receiving feedback. Income is often unpredictable in the early years, which is worth knowing going in rather than discovering by surprise.

BSID-001 — BS Interior Design

Overview: Interior Design trains students to plan and design indoor spaces — homes, offices, restaurants, hotels — balancing visual appeal with practical function for the people using the space. Coursework covers materials, space planning, and building codes, translating a client's needs into a functional, cohesive interior. Practice is regulated by law (Republic Act No. 10350, the Philippine Interior Design Act of 2012): graduates must pass the PRC Interior Design Licensure Exam with a general weighted average of at least 70% before they can legally use the title "Interior Designer" or sign off on design plans.

Where Can You Study:

University of Santo Tomas, Manila — Private, national reference
Ateneo de Naga University, if offered under a related design program, Naga City, Camarines Sur — Private
Elsewhere: De La Salle-College of Saint Benilde is known nationally for strong Interior Design programs.

Where Can This Path Lead:

Junior Interior Designer — ₱18,000–₱28,000/mo entry-level
Furniture/Space Planner — ₱16,000–₱25,000/mo entry-level
Design Firm Drafter/CAD Operator — ₱16,000–₱24,000/mo entry-level, a common entry job while building experience and reviewing for the boards
Freelance Residential Designer — variable income, common once a client base has been built

Guidance Tip: Software skills such as AutoCAD and SketchUp, alongside a strong portfolio, matter a great deal here, so free tutorials or school electives in design software are worth pursuing early. Since licensure is legally required to use the professional title, factor board exam review time into your college planning from the start.

BSMA-001 — BS Multimedia Arts

Overview: Multimedia Arts covers digital content creation — graphic design, animation, video editing, and increasingly UI/UX and game art — using industry-standard software. It's a hands-on, project-heavy course where a demo reel or portfolio often matters more to employers than grades. Demand for digital content (social media, advertising, games, streaming) has grown substantially, and the skill set opens doors into traditional media production as well as freelance and remote/global gig work.

Where Can You Study:

Where Can This Path Lead:

Video Editor/Animator — ₱18,000–₱30,000/mo entry-level
UI/UX Designer (with additional skill-building) — ₱20,000–₱32,000/mo entry-level, a growing entry point for graduates who lean toward digital product design
Freelance Digital Artist/Content Creator — variable income, increasingly common through freelance platforms and social media work

Guidance Tip: A portfolio or design and animation reel is weighed more heavily than grades when applying for jobs in this field, so every school project is worth treating as something to showcase later rather than simply submit and forget. Freelance platforms such as Upwork, Fiverr, and local Facebook groups for creatives are realistic entry points even before graduation.
